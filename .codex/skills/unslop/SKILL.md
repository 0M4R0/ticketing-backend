# Unslop

Edit the supplied text to remove the patterns below. Preserve its meaning and match the intended tone. Apply rules that fit the text; do not distort a sentence just to satisfy a rule mechanically.

Rule numbers are stable IDs that other skills can cite. If a rule is removed, leave its number unused.

3. Avoid trailing "-ing" phrases that add no fact, such as "highlighting..." or "ensuring...".
5. Replace vague attributions such as "experts believe" with a named source, or delete them.
7. Avoid these AI-associated words: additionally, crucial, delve, enhance, foster, landscape, pivotal, showcase, testament, underscore, vibrant.
8. Prefer "is" or "has" over "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", or "features" when they mean the same thing.
9. State the point directly. Avoid "not just X, but Y" constructions.
10. Do not force ideas into groups of three.
11. Use one name for each thing. Repeat that name.
12. Use "from X to Y" only when X and Y sit on a real scale.
13. Do not use em dashes, en dashes, or hyphens as dashes. Use a period or comma instead. Do not replace a dash with parentheses.
14. Use a colon before a list or example, not as a mid-sentence connector.
15. Do not bold every proper noun or acronym.
16. Avoid bold labels followed by a colon that restate the next sentence. A bold lead-in ending in a period is fine when it adds detail.
17. Use sentence case for headings.
18. Do not use decorative emojis.
19. Use straight quotation marks.
20. Remove chatbot phrases such as "I hope this helps!" and "Let me know if...".
22. Start with the response. Do not open with praise or agreement.
23. Cut filler. Change "in order to" to "to" and delete phrases such as "it is important to note that".
24. Use at most one hedge: "may".
25. End with a specific plan or fact, not a generic conclusion.
26. Replace abstract metaphor nouns such as substrate, wedge, vector, nexus, primitive, harness, surface, scaffolding, paradigm, ratchet, north star, and flywheel with concrete words.
27. Name the mechanism or number. Remove generic sentences that could appear unchanged in another project's documentation.
28. Keep one idea per sentence. Split sentences that readers have to reread.
29. Prefer active voice. Name the actor unless it is unknown or does not matter.
30. Cut adverbs or use a stronger verb. Give the number when a number is available.
31. Prefer plain words, such as "use" over "utilize" or "leverage".
32. Remove metaphors, aphorisms, personified code, and figurative verbs when literal wording works.
33. Write complete sentences with articles and verbs. Spell out arrows and abbreviations.
